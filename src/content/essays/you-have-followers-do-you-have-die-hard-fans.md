People need to buy you, before they buy your thing.

I would argue a founder's personal brand is now more important than the product's/company's brand.

Look at the hottest brands right now -

-   Rhode IS Hailey Bieber
-   Rare Beauty IS Selena Gomez

A great startup example is [Chloe Walsh](https://www.tiktok.com/@chloerebeccawalsh), founder of Clomana - they sell beauty sponges that I have no clue what you do with, but girls love them.

Chloe’s nan gave her £530 to start the brand, Which Chloe has now turned into a £30m beauty empire. Rockstar.

But that’s not the thing to pay attention to. The thing to pay attention to is:

‘How the hell did i know the story about Chloe’s nan??’

Because every new person that lands on Clomana's TikTok learns that story.

<img src="/images/essays/clomana.webp" alt="Clomaná’s TikTok profile beside its pinned post of the founder with her nana, captioned “Nana, please can you help me start my own business? It’s £530 for 100 pairs of lashes?”" width="1079" height="1177" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

The audience sees the sponge, but they also see the whole character arc leading up to that sponge.

Clomana now has a fanbase that will show up and engage with anything Chloe puts out - That's real longevity.

Get people to buy the lore first, then the product.
