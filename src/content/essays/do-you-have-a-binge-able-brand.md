Anyone remember the 2015 era of YouTube daily vloggers?

My favourite was BFvsGF.

Millions of passionate, dedicated people would wait for Jesse & Jeana's upload every single day.

Then they broke up… That was sad ☹️

But that raw, behind-the-scenes format is still probably the best way to build an audience that genuinely roots for you. Even in 2026.

And a few smart founders have adapted it for short-form.

Best example is Kanita, founder of [BrownieGod](https://tiktok.com/@browniegod).

In 3 years, she's sold over 3M brownies through TikTok and has grown to over 600k followers and 25M likes.

If you look at other brownie sellers online, their content is pretty much all the same:

Aesthetic edits of finished brownies. No vlogging or peek into the process how they were made.

Kanita started off her account by doing that (filter for oldest and you can see the early content), but quickly realised that wouldn’t make her brand stand out in the sea of other bakery brands.

So she took a leaf out of the daily vlogger’s book and pivoted to vlogging the entire process, literally from from ‘sack of chocolate’ to ‘box of brownies’ landing on someone's doorstep.

Go read the comments on her videos.

The level of love people have for this brand is mad.

You build that by showing up every day and letting people into the process.

Not just the polished finished product, but the real, authentic process of how you got there - That’s what daily vloggers did in 2015 and what Kanita is doing to this day.

Formats don’t ever really die. They just go out of season until someone adapts it and brings it back.

And daily vlogging is so back.

— Danny
