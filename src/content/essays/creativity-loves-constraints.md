We often think ‘ahh if I just had more X, i would be able to do Yʼ

How many times have you thought:

-   If I just had more time, I could go to the gym everyday!
-   If I just had more money, I could get that girl!
-   If I just had more Instagram followers, I could quit my job and start that brand!

You get the point. The list goes on.

Itʼs not just the ‘ambitious procrastinatorʼ that thinks like this. We all think like this about something in our lives.

> ‘Well Danny, we think like this because itʼs true!!ʼ

Is it?

I mean sure, in theory if you did have more free time in your day, you could theoretically fit the gym in more consistently.

If you did have a little more money, you could theoretically quit your job because you can pay your bills from savings while starting that business.

This all sounds great. But in reality, humans donʼt work this way.

Weʼre all hardwired to be lazy as f\*\*\* and so giving us more time in the day does not equate to us being maximally productive with that additional time.

If it did, the people with the most open schedules would drive innovation and the people with the busiest calendars would be the least innovative.

Reality shows us the opposite… But why is that?

Simple:

## Creativity loves constraints

Some of the best things humanity has ever created, have not come from a team with infinite resources.

Quite the opposite - They usually come from a team thatʼs incredibly resource constrained and has to find creative solutions to that scarcity.

Back in the early days of SpaceX, Elon wouldnʼt bat an eyelid spending 100k for a new Epoxy floor, but in the same day, would reject his engineers request to buy a critical machine for a few thousand dollars.

His reason would be ‘this machine is built to make screws at X cost, but we need screws at a fraction of that cost. no.ʼ

His team would be infuriated with his generosity in things that donʼt innovate the product )like the hangarʼs flooring), contrasted with his stinginess in buying cheap machinery that would save engineerʼs months of time.

But what was the method to that madness? Well SpaceX created a rocket at a tiny fraction of the cost that the rest of the market produced at + they made their rockets reusable to drive the cost even lower.

The resource constraint literally caused SpaceX to build a rocket at an infintely smaller cost than all other teams building rockets that fly once and then blow up.

Elon knew that creativity loves constraints, so he created artificial scarcity to drive innovation.

Steve Jobs agrees - He said that a finished product can be defined as a basket of compromises.

Both of these guys built multiple, deca-billion dollar companies. Elon with Tesla & SpaceX and Jobs with Apple & Pixar.

You wouldʼve thought that just the one deca-unicorn would take up all of their time… But somehow even with a ruthlessly limited time budget, they built more than one deca-unicorn.

So the moral of the story:

Donʼt feel hard done by the scarcity of X resource that you feel is holding back your progress in whatever you want to achieve.

If you look through the lens of the best innovators humanity has given us, often times you realise that the scarcity forces you to be creative and at the end of that road is where the best results live.
